When Long Island Homeowners Should Rekey, Not Replace

If you just closed on a home in Garden City or Hempstead, you have no way of knowing how many keys the previous owner handed out over the years — to contractors, house sitters, or old tenants. Rekeying closes that gap without replacing hardware that's already in good shape.

It's also the right move after a lost key, a break-in attempt, or a roommate or relationship change anywhere in Levittown and beyond — any situation where you can't account for every key that opens your door.

How Lock Rekeying Works

Our technician opens the existing cylinder, replaces the internal pins to match a new key, and tests it on the spot — the outside of your lock looks and functions exactly the same, but every old key stops working immediately.

If you have multiple doors, we can rekey all of them to a single new key so you're not juggling separate keys for the front door, back door, and garage.

Rekeying Cost & How Long It Lasts

Rekeying typically runs $25–$50 per cylinder, with most homes landing around $100–$200 total for a full-house rekey — a fraction of what replacing all that hardware would cost.

A rekey holds until you decide to rekey again; there's no wear-related expiration. Most homeowners only rekey again after another move-in, lost key, or change in who has access.

Why Skipping a Rekey After a Move Is a Risk

The most overlooked security step after moving into a Long Island home isn't a new alarm system — it's simply not knowing who else has a working key to your front door. That risk sits there quietly until something happens.

It's also one of the cheapest fixes on this list. Skipping a $100–$200 rekey to save money now is a bad trade against the cost, stress, and disruption of dealing with unauthorized entry later.

FAQ

Common Questions About Lock Rekeying on Long Island

What's the difference between rekeying and replacing a lock?

Rekeying changes the internal pins so old keys no longer work, while keeping your existing lock hardware. Replacement swaps the entire lock. Rekeying is faster and cheaper when your hardware is already in good condition.

Can you make all my doors use one key?

Yes — we can rekey multiple locks to a single key during the same visit, which is a common request for front doors, back doors, and garage entries.
