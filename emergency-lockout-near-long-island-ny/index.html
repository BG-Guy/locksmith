Locked Out on Long Island? Here's Why It Happens

A lockout rarely happens at a convenient time. Most calls we get from Hempstead, Levittown, and Garden City come in late at night, right after a move, or the moment someone's rushing out the door for work with keys still on the kitchen counter.

Cold weather makes it worse — older deadbolts around Long Island tend to stick or freeze in winter, and a key that turns fine in October can jam solid by January. Car lockouts spike in summer, when key fobs get left in beach bags or trunks.

How Our Long Island Lockout Technicians Get You Back In

Every technician we send carries a full lockout kit and tries non-destructive entry first — picking, bypassing, or manipulating the existing lock so your hardware stays intact. Drilling is a last resort, not the default.

You'll get a flat-rate quote over the phone before anyone is dispatched, and the nearest available, ID-verified technician heads straight to your address — no call center relay, no guessing who's showing up.

What a Lockout Call Costs on Long Island

Most residential and vehicle lockouts across Nassau and Suffolk County run $75–$150, depending on the time of day and lock type. Commercial lockouts and high-security cylinders can run higher — we'll always confirm the number before starting work, not after.

There's no long-term maintenance involved with a lockout itself, but if the lock stuck or failed because it's worn out, we'll flag it on the spot so you're not calling us back next month for the same door.

Why Waiting on a Lockout Can Cost You More

Standing outside doesn't just cost you time — a door left unattended, especially at night, is a window of opportunity for anyone walking by. Cars parked and unlocked-looking for an hour attract the wrong kind of attention too.

Trying to force entry yourself is the bigger risk: bent frames, cracked window seals, and snapped keys inside the cylinder turn a simple $100 lockout into a $400+ repair. A quick call gets you back inside without turning one problem into two.
