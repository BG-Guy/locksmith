Why Standard Deadbolts Aren't Enough on Long Island

Most builder-grade deadbolts installed in homes across Smithtown and Huntington were never designed to resist bumping or picking — techniques that are widely known and take an experienced person under a minute to execute on standard pin-tumbler locks.

For businesses, and for homes in Massapequa with rental units or multiple tenants over the years, restricted keyways solve a different problem: keys that can't be duplicated at a hardware store without your authorization.

How We Upgrade You to High-Security Hardware

We assess your existing entry points first — a high-security deadbolt on a weak door frame or thin strike plate still leaves an easy way in, so reinforcement often goes hand-in-hand with the lock upgrade itself.

For restricted keyway systems, we set up your account with the manufacturer so future key duplication has to go through us or an authorized dealer, giving you actual control over who can get a copy.

High-Security Lock Pricing & Lifespan

A high-security deadbolt upgrade typically runs $200–$600 per door depending on the hardware grade and whether frame reinforcement is included. Safes are quoted separately based on size and fire rating.

This hardware is built to last significantly longer than standard-grade locks — often 20+ years — and the restricted keyway means you won't need to rekey every time you're unsure who has a copy.

What's at Risk With Standard-Grade Locks

Bump keys and basic picks are inexpensive and easy to find, which means a determined person doesn't need to break a window or kick a door — a standard lock can simply be opened quietly, leaving no obvious sign of forced entry.

For rental properties or businesses across Long Island where you can't track every key ever copied over the years, that uncertainty doesn't go away on its own. A high-security upgrade is a one-time fix instead of an ongoing risk.
